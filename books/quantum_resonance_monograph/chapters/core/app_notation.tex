\label{app:notation}

The principal symbols are collected here for reference.  The Hamiltonian is
$H$, its free or asymptotic reference is $H_0$, and the complex-scaled operator
is $H_\theta$.  The resolvent is $\Resolvent(z)=(z-H)^{-1}$.  A resonance
energy is $\resonantE=E_*-\rmi\Gamma/2$, with width $\Gamma>0$.  The
resolvent set, spectrum, essential spectrum, absolutely continuous spectrum,
and $\epsilon$-pseudospectrum are denoted by $\resolventset(H)$,
$\spectrum(H)$, $\essspectrum(H)$, $\acspectrum(H)$, and
$\pseudospectrum(H)$, respectively.  The bounded operators on $\Hil$ form
$\BHil$.  The spectral projections are $P(\Delta)$, and
$\int_X^\oplus\Hil_x\dd\mu(x)$ denotes a direct integral over the measure
space $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$.  The physical scattering operator is
$\Scattering=\Omega^{(-)\dagger}\Omega^{(+)}$ and its on-shell fiber is
$\Scattering(E)$.  Calligraphic symbols such as $\mathcal S_\vartheta$ are
reserved for directional Borel summation in exact WKB and are not scattering
operators.  The observable $C^*$ algebra is $\Alg$, a \vN\
algebra is $\vNAlg$, and its center is $\Center(\vNAlg)$.  The wave number is
$k$; for Coulomb scattering the Sommerfeld parameter is
$\eta=\mu g/(\hbar^2k)$.  A black-hole QNM frequency is $\omega$ and has
spectral energy $E=\omega^2$.  The exact-WKB Riccati function is $S$, its odd
part is $\vsub{S}{odd}$, a Voros period is $\VorosPeriod_\gamma$, and its exponential
is $\VorosExp_\gamma$.  The incoming connection datum is always written
$\Cincoming$.  It is a scalar coefficient in a one-channel problem and the
corresponding incoming block in a multichannel problem; these are two
dimensions of the same connection object, not two distinct concepts.  Its
zero is interpreted with the domain, sheet, and boundary condition already
fixed.  The test space, Hilbert space, and antidual form the
triple $\PhiSpace\subset\Hil\subset\PhiSpace^\times$.  Right and left
eigenvectors carry superscripts $\mathrm R$ and~$\mathrm L$.  A control
parameter near an exceptional point (EP) is $\lambda$, and the EP is at~$\vsub{\lambda}{EP}$.
